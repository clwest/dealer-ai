# Session handoff — Sales behavior system as it exists today

This handoff is a **state-of-the-system snapshot**, not a "what shipped this
session" log. It describes the AI Sales Assistant's behavior surfaces as
they actually exist in the code, so the next session can act on real state
instead of stale phase narratives. Source-of-truth code references are
inline (`file:line`).

The behavior layer governs *how the assistant sounds, looks, and respects
prior turns*. The deterministic backend governs *what's actually true*
(prices, payments, classifications, lever picks). The LLM is a language
layer over both — it explains, it does not decide.

---

## 1. Sales behavior system — card + conversational layer

The customer chat reply is **two surfaces in one turn**:

- **Cards** — `matched_vehicles[]` rendered by
  `frontend/src/components/ChatVehicleCard.tsx`. Authoritative for price,
  estimated monthly payment (W.A.C.), Stock #, mileage, drivetrain,
  condition, budget-fit badge, and (when applicable) lever-flex badge +
  explainer caption.
- **Assistant prose** — generated by the LLM over a per-branch reply
  rule emitted by `_format_budget_block` in
  `backend/dealer_ai/services/chat_engine.py`. The rule is prepended with
  `_CARD_PRESENTATION_PREAMBLE` (`chat_engine.py:2708`) on every
  card-bearing branch.

The contract between the two surfaces is enforced at three points:

1. **Pre-LLM.** The reply rule names exactly which lead vehicle to
   foreground, which stretches/flexes are real, and what soft-close
   pattern to use. The preamble ships a verbatim GOOD example and three
   BAD examples — small models imitate examples more reliably than they
   follow rule lists.
2. **Post-LLM scrubs.** `check_payment_consistency` (chat_engine.py:1607)
   flags any `$X/mo` not in `allowed_payments` (assembled from
   `matched_vehicles[*].estimated_payment` ± $5). Stock #s the LLM cites
   that don't appear in `matched_vehicles` trigger
   `FABRICATED_INVENTORY_RESPONSE` (`chat_engine.py:614`,
   `chat_engine.py:3831`). Rate language is stripped (`scrub_rate_language`).
3. **Frontend.** Only `matched_vehicles[*]` renders cards.
   `ChatBubble.tsx` does not parse free text into specs — if the assistant
   prose mentions a vehicle that isn't on a card, the customer sees prose
   only, not a card.

### Voice / tone contract (load-bearing phrasings)

These phrasings are anchored in `tests/test_stretch_options.py`,
`tests/test_lever_accept.py`, and `tests/test_card_presentation_rules.py`.
Changing them breaks tests:

- `The [year] [model] is really close at about $X/mo` — opener for
  near-fit and lever-flex branches.
- `The [year] [model] is the closest match I have at about $X/mo` —
  opener for single-near and single-stretch branches.
- `Would you be open to adjusting one of those so I can show you more
  options?` — verbatim soft close for single-near / single-stretch
  branches; `"options"` may be replaced with the obvious category
  (`"more trucks"`, `"more SUVs"`).
- Three upsell phrases, LLM picks ONE: *"if you're open to stretching
  just a bit, that opens up …"* | *"that opens up options like …"* |
  *"with a little flexibility, you could step into …"*.
- Lever-flex multi-lever close: `_lever_flex_close_question(kinds_present)`
  (`chat_engine.py:2749`) — only mentions levers that actually yielded
  cards.

### Sentence + payment-quote caps

- 3–5 sentences when ≥2 cards are present.
- Exactly **one** payment quote in prose, and only for the lead vehicle.
  Other cards are referenced qualitatively (`"bigger truck"`,
  `"newer year"`, `"gets you under budget"`).
- No bullet lists, no numbered steps, no pipe-delimited spec dumps.

## 2. Stretch + lever logic

`_format_budget_block` runs after `build_budget_context` returns three
buckets (`matched_in_budget`, `near_fit`, `closest_above`) and a fourth
bucket (`lever_flex_options`). Stretches and lever-flex picks are both
ways to surface real inventory the customer can reach if they bend a
constraint, but they're computed differently and labeled differently.

### Realistic-stretch ceiling

Constants in `chat_engine.py`:

- `MULTI_OPTION_TOTAL_CAP = 3` — total cards per turn.
- `STRETCH_FLOOR_DOLLARS = 150.0`, `STRETCH_PERCENT_OF_TARGET = 0.30` —
  `max_stretch_delta = max(150, 0.30 × target)`. Anything above target
  by more than this is "different car class," not a stretch.

Stretches come from `closest_above` (over-budget pool sorted by
ascending payment delta). They are **promoted into `matched_vehicles[]`**
so they render as full cards (with the `Above target · +$X/mo` badge)
and so the fabricated-inventory guard's allow-list naturally widens to
permit citing their Stock #s.

### Lever-flex picker

`_pick_lever_flex_options` (`chat_engine.py:2418`) fills any spare slots
inside the cap of 3 with vehicles the customer can reach by **flexing
exactly one lever** from their stated ask. Three lever kinds, evaluated
in order:

1. **`longer_term`** — re-classify at `next_term_suggestion(term)` (e.g.
   60 → 72; 72 → 84). Keep vehicles whose new delta ≤ realistic-stretch
   cap. Caption: `Needs N-mo term (vs current M-mo)`.
2. **`more_down`** — re-classify at `down + $2k`, then `down + $5k`
   (`_DOWN_BUMPS`). Keep vehicles whose new delta ≤ realistic-stretch
   cap. Caption: `Needs $X down (vs current $Y)`.
3. **`drivetrain_flex`** — only fires when the profile carries a strict
   drivetrain (`4WD` / `AWD` / `RWD` / `FWD`). Re-classify with the
   drivetrain filter dropped, then drop vehicles that already satisfy
   the strict ask (those are plain matches, not "flex"). Caption:
   `This is 2WD — flexible-drivetrain option (your ask: 4WD)`.

Each pick is annotated by `_annotate_flex` with `_lever_flex_kind`,
`_lever_flex_explainer`, and the lever's specific knobs
(`_lever_flex_term_months`, `_lever_flex_down_payment`,
`_lever_flex_drivetrain_required`). The `VehicleSerializer` exposes
these to the frontend.

### Branch matrix

`_format_budget_block` chooses one of these branches based on the four
counts. `has_lever_flex` always wins when flex picks exist:

| Trigger | Branch | Lever offer? | Soft close |
|---|---|---|---|
| no cards anywhere | `else` | no | EXACTLY ONE narrowing question |
| `fit_count > 0` + stretches | `fit_count > 0 and has_stretches` | no | warm next-step |
| `fit_count > 0` only | `fit_count > 0` | no | warm next-step |
| `near_count > 0` + stretches | `near_count > 0 and has_stretches` | no | "Would that be something you'd consider?" |
| exactly 1 near, no stretch | `near_count == 1` | **yes** | verbatim "Would you be open to adjusting one of those…" |
| 2+ near, no stretch | `near_count > 0` (multi) | no | EXACTLY ONE narrowing question |
| no fit, no near, exactly 1 stretch | `fit_count == 0 and len(closest_above) == 1` | **yes** | verbatim "Would you be open to adjusting one of those…" |
| any branch + flex picks | `has_lever_flex` (highest priority) | **yes** | dynamic multi-lever close |

The `lever_offer=True` flag is set on the assistant message metadata
when the branch is single-near, single-stretch, or any flex-bearing
turn (`chat_engine.py:3962`). This flag is what the next turn's
lever-accept flow keys off.

## 3. Lever-accept flow

When the assistant's prior turn was a lever offer (`lever_offer=True`),
the customer's next message routes through one of three handlers
**before** intent extraction or any LLM call. This avoids re-running the
budget search on a guess.

The router lives at the top of `handle_user_message`
(`chat_engine.py:3499`–`3554`):

### Router 1 — Numberless lever ask

`lever_intent(user_text)` (`intent_parser.py:695`) returns:

- `"longer_term"` — for *"try a longer term"*, *"longer loan please"*.
  Routes to `_longer_term_clarifier_response(current_term)`
  (`chat_engine.py:788`). Term-aware: at 84+ months it does NOT propose
  a longer term — it redirects to "more down, trade-in, or flexible
  drivetrain."
- `"more_down"` — for *"I can put more down"*, *"bigger down payment"*.
  Routes to `MORE_DOWN_CLARIFIER_RESPONSE` (`chat_engine.py:782`):
  *"Got it — how much down can you go to?"*
- `None` — message either named a specific number (defer to
  `regex_extract` → normal pipeline) or didn't ask for a lever.

The router persists a fresh assistant message with
`metadata.mode="lever_clarifier"`, `metadata.lever_clarifier_kind=<kind>`,
`metadata.lever_offer=True`, `metadata.provider="guard"`. No LLM call.

### Router 2 — Bare confirmation

`is_bare_confirmation(user_text)` (`intent_parser.py:662`) matches `yes`,
`yeah`, `sure`, `ok`, `sounds good`, `let's try it`, etc. When it fires
**and** the prior assistant message had `metadata.lever_offer=True`,
returns `LEVER_CLARIFIER_RESPONSE` (`chat_engine.py:774`):

> *"Great — which would you like to try first: a longer term, more down,
> a trade-in, or a more flexible drivetrain?"*

Bare "yes" without a preceding lever offer falls through to the normal
pipeline — other flows (appointment confirmation, etc.) handle it.

### Router 3 — Specific number named

When the customer responds with a specific number (*"let's try 84 months"*,
*"I can do $5,000 down"*), `regex_extract` picks it up, `merge_profile`
overwrites the prior value, and the normal budget pipeline reruns at
the new constraint. No clarifier fires.

### Why this matters

`merge_profile` is **additive only** — empty values never overwrite
known data. Combined with the `_CURRENCY_SIGNAL_RE` hallucination guard
(strips LLM-emitted `target_monthly_payment` / `down_payment` /
`max_price` when no currency cue was in the message), the lever-accept
clarifiers are the only safe path to update a constraint mid-conversation.
Skipping them means the small Ollama model can silently misroute a bare
"yes" into a search with the wrong profile.

## 4. Behavior layer integration

The behavior layer integrates with the deterministic stack at four
documented seams. Each one has a code anchor and a test file.

| Seam | Where | Code | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card presentation preamble | Prepended to every card-bearing branch in `_format_budget_block` | `chat_engine.py:2708` | `test_card_presentation_rules.py` |
| Lever-flex picker + annotations | `_pick_lever_flex_options`, `_annotate_flex` | `chat_engine.py:2418`, `:2381` | `test_lever_flex_presentation.py` |
| Lever-accept routing | Pre-intent handlers at top of `handle_user_message` | `chat_engine.py:3499`–`3554` | `test_lever_accept.py` |
| Stretch promotion | Realistic-stretch cap + promotion to `matched_vehicles` | `chat_engine.py:2217`–`2230` | `test_stretch_options.py` |

The behavior layer also relies on:

- **`_CURRENCY_SIGNAL_RE`** in `intent_parser.py` — drops hallucinated
  numeric profile fields from the LLM's intent JSON when no currency cue
  exists in the message.
- **`scrub_rate_language`**, **`scrub_internal_directives`**,
  **`scrub_default_assumption_language`**, **`scrub_budget_category_labels`**
  — partial post-LLM scrubs that strip W.A.C.-violating phrasings, leaked
  directive prose, "with no money down" assumptions, and "nearly in
  budget" / "slightly above budget" rebadging.
- **`detect_unsafe_response`**, **`detect_internal_confusion`**,
  **`scrub_post_llm_override`** — wholesale-replacement scrubs that fire
  when the model leaked dealer-cost language, dumped guideline prose, or
  agreed to a discount/handoff the deterministic layer didn't authorize.
- **Shared `services/llm_safety.py`** — the partial scrubs are reused by
  `chat_engine`, `vehicle_assistant`, `ad_copy`, and `follow_up`. Per
  `docs/PROJECT_PIPELINE.md` §10 the post-LLM-scrub asymmetry between
  chat and vehicle-ask is closed via this shared helper. (See §6 below
  — this is one of the items the next session should re-verify against
  the runtime, since `context/INVENTORY.md` still describes vehicle-ask
  as scrub-less.)

## 5. Frontend card rendering + badges

`frontend/src/components/ChatVehicleCard.tsx` (Path 1, `matched`
present) renders:

- Image (or `no photo` placeholder)
- Display name + price (top row)
- Stock # + badges (budget-fit, lever-flex, condition) + mileage chip +
  drivetrain chip (chip row)
- Estimated monthly payment in W.A.C. format (no APR column)
- Lever-flex explainer caption (italic, when present)

### Badges

**Budget-fit badge** (`budgetFitBadge`, `ChatVehicleCard.tsx:43`):

| `budget_fit` | Label | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| `fit` | `In budget` | emerald |
| `near_fit` | `Close · +$X/mo` (or `Close to target` if delta missing) | amber |
| `over_budget` | `Above target · +$X/mo` (or `Above target`) | rose |

`over_budget` only renders when the backend promoted a stretch into
`matched_vehicles[]` (delta ≤ realistic-stretch cap). The cap means
customers never see a `+$400/mo above target` card.

**Lever-flex badge** (`leverFlexBadge`, `ChatVehicleCard.tsx:82`):

| `lever_flex_kind` | Label | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| `longer_term` | `Longer term` | sky |
| `more_down` | `More down` | sky |
| `drivetrain_flex` | `Drivetrain flex` | violet |
| `stretch_payment` | `Stretch` | rose |

The two badges render side-by-side. A flex card with `over_budget` +
`longer_term` shows both `Above target · +$X/mo` and `Longer term`.

### Path 2 — parsed-only fallback

When the assistant prose names a vehicle that's not in
`matched_vehicles[]`, `ChatVehicleCard` falls back to a minimal card
with parsed display text only — no specs, no badges. This is intentional:
prose-only mentions are not authoritative and must not look like cards.

### Frontend API contract

`frontend/src/lib/api.ts` (Vehicle interface at `lib/api.ts:31+`):

- `budget_fit?: "fit" | "near_fit" | "over_budget" | null`
- `payment_delta?: number | null`
- `lever_flex_kind?: "longer_term" | "more_down" | "drivetrain_flex" | "stretch_payment" | null`
- `lever_flex_explainer?: string | null`

The serializer (`backend/dealer_ai/serializers.py`) is the only path
these fields take from `Vehicle` instance attrs (set by the chat engine's
classifier + flex picker) into the API response.

### Edge cases in lever-accept flow

- **Bare "yes" with no preceding lever offer.** Falls through to the
  normal pipeline. Other flows (appointment confirmation, "yes I'd
  like an advisor to call") have to handle it themselves. Make sure
  any new flow that asks a yes/no question either sets
  `lever_offer=True` (so the clarifier fires) or has its own bare-
  confirmation handler upstream of the budget pipeline.
- **84+ month current term + "longer term" lever.** The longer-term
  clarifier (`chat_engine.py:788`) refuses to suggest an even longer
  term and redirects to a different lever. Tests pin this. If the
  customer is at 96 months it's the same redirect — don't add a
  "stretch to 120 months" code path.
- **Drivetrain flex requires a strict drivetrain in profile.** If the
  customer never named a drivetrain, the picker won't surface
  drivetrain-flex cards because there's nothing to flex from. This is
  intentional — but it means a customer who said *"truck around $500/mo"*
  (no drivetrain) and gets a single near-fit will see a single-near
  branch with no flex picks even when 2WD trucks exist that would fit
  exactly. The fix (if ever desired) is at the picker, not at the
  branch: surface "drivetrain isn't constraining you here" instead of
  treating "no constraint" as "no flex possible."

### Constraint-preservation edge cases

- **`merge_profile` is additive only.** The only way to clear a
  constraint mid-session is a sentinel value (`drivetrain="any"` is
  the only one currently implemented). Adding a new clearable
  constraint requires both a release sentinel and a release-phrase
  regex in `intent_parser.py`.
- **`_CURRENCY_SIGNAL_RE` strips numeric LLM hallucinations.** If a
  future profile field is numeric (e.g., `mileage_cap`), it must be
  added to the same guard list or the small model will inherit the
  same hallucination risk that bit `target_monthly_payment` early on.
- **Anchor reset on category change.** Naming a new model OR a new
  vehicle_type clears `current_vehicle_id` / `current_vehicle_stock`
  in `merge_profile`. This is what stops "the same Ranger" from
  bleeding into a follow-up about an SUV. If a new "anchor" field is
  added, the reset rule has to be extended too.

Five items from the post-`SESSION_002` polish backlog landed in this
sequence. Each is a self-contained post-LLM enforcer that runs in the
order shown below — they compose, and the metadata flag chain
promotes to ``multiple_scrubs_fired`` whenever ≥ 2 fire on the same
turn. Code lives in `backend/dealer_ai/services/chat_engine.py`,
tests in `backend/dealer_ai/tests/`. Test count: **880 pass, 1
skipped, 0 failed** (was 799 before this pass — +81 new tests across
six suites).

### 9.2 The system now enforces behavior post-LLM, not prompt-only

This is the load-bearing structural change. Before this pass, the
``_CARD_PRESENTATION_PREAMBLE`` carried the full presentation
contract as prompt directives; the small Ollama model imitated
the `BAD` examples verbatim and ignored most of the negative rules.
The contract is now enforced at two layers:

- **Prompt-time** — preamble + branch-specific reply rules tell the
  model what shape to produce.
- **Post-generation** — six independent scrubs (above) verify every
  load-bearing contract clause and replace / strip whatever the
  model failed to honor.

Concretely, the rules previously documented as "the LLM must..."
now have post-generation enforcers:

| Behavior contract | Pre-pass enforcement | Post-pass enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| One payment quote per turn | prompt rule (BEHAVIOR_LAYER §"One-payment-quote rule") | `scrub_extra_payment_quotes` |
| No fabricated payments | prompt rule + `check_payment_consistency` (audit only) | `scrub_payment_drift` (action) |
| No bullet/pipe shape when cards exist | prompt rule (`_CARD_PRESENTATION_PREAMBLE`) | `scrub_list_shape` |
| One natural close question | prompt rule | `scrub_followup_question` |
| No meta narration | prompt rule + `internal_confusion_fallback` (wholesale) | `scrub_meta_narration` (partial strip) |
| BAD examples don't contaminate output | prompt rule | preamble BAD example numerically sanitised |

This is the principle BEHAVIOR_LAYER §"Small-Model Behavior Note"
already articulated: *"any behavior contract here that's
load-bearing for safety, accuracy, or compliance must have a
post-generation check in code."* The pass extended that principle
from compliance-only (rate / directive / category) to the full
presentation contract.

  Performance**` / `**Interior and Features**` markdown headings and
  tab-indented sub-bullets.
- Scenario 5 wrapped the reply in `"Here's a revised response that
  takes into account..."` and trailed with `"(Note: I've removed the
  specific payment quote…)"`.

**After this pass:** the smoke confirms each of those failure modes
is now intercepted post-generation. Scenario 2 lands at the
`LIST_SHAPE_FALLBACK` when the LLM produces a heavy spec dump;
scenario 3 still produces verbose prose but no bullet/heading shape;
scenario 5's meta wrapper is stripped by `scrub_meta_narration`.

The `llama3.2` 3.2B model — half the size of the documented
`llama3.1` 8B default — is **viable for the customer-facing chat**
because the enforcement layer guarantees what the prompt cannot. If
the model is upgraded to llama3.1 / 3.3 / Mistral / GPT-4o-mini,
the scrubs become belt-and-suspenders rather than primary
enforcement, and the overall surface quality improves further.
This decouples model choice from contract guarantees.

### 9.4 Known remaining issue: drivetrain / feature hallucination

The one open class from the cross-cutting list is **item 4 — card-
claims fact-check**. The smoke surfaced it in two shapes:

- **Drivetrain hallucination.** The LLM occasionally claims a card's
  drivetrain is something other than what the card shows — e.g.,
  *"the Colorado is available in both 2WD and 4WD configurations"*
  for a card whose `drivetrain` field is `4x2` only. The
  `FABRICATED_INVENTORY_RESPONSE` guard only covers Stock #s, not
  attribute claims.
- **Feature / spec hallucination.** Inline markdown bold like
  `"**Engine:** 2.3L EcoBoost..."` survives `scrub_list_shape`
  (which only catches bold lines that ARE the whole line) and
  carries claims about engine, transmission, towing, fuel economy,
  paint, and feature lists. Some of these claims aren't in the
  card's `features` array — they're invented to fill out the prose.

Neither is a payment- or compliance-grade safety issue (no money,
no rate, no Stock # involved), so they're lower-priority than the
five items that just landed. But they're the next visible
correctness gap.
